From mass to separation factor

One effective harmonic mode in each environment.

01 Convert molar mass to oscillator mass

Mi=mi×10−3NA

mi is in g mol⁻¹; Mi is in kg per oscillator. NA is Avogadro’s constant. Multiplying by 10⁻³ alone gives kg mol⁻¹, so division by NA is essential.

02 Calculate zero-point energies

Ei,s=ℏ2FsMi,Gi,s≈NAEi,s

i = L or H; s = A or B. Fs is in N m⁻¹. E is in J per oscillator; G is approximated by the molar ZPE in J mol⁻¹. ℏ = h/(2π).

03 Evaluate the isotope-exchange energy

HA + LB ⇌ HB + LA
ΔΔG=(GH,B−GH,A)−(GL,B−GL,A)

Here ΔΔG is the standard molar free energy of the written exchange, approximated solely by its ZPE contribution. A negative value favors heavy-isotope transfer from A to B.

04 Convert energy to an equilibrium ratio

αB/A=(H/L)B(H/L)A≈exp(−ΔΔGRT)

T is in kelvin and R = NAkB. The isotope ratios are interpreted as activity ratios, or composition ratios for ideal mixing. Exchanging A and B gives the reciprocal factor.

What this estimate includes

This is a single-mode, harmonic, ZPE-only approximation to equilibrium isotope fractionation. Each state has one positive force constant, shared by the two isotopes; the effective mass pair is the same in both states. For a diatomic stretch, use the reduced molar mass μ = m₁m₂/(m₁ + m₂) for each isotopologue.

Thermally excited vibrations, other normal modes, rotational and translational terms, anharmonicity, and nonideal activities are omitted. These can change the magnitude and even the direction of actual fractionation. The result is an equilibrium estimate, not a kinetic isotope effect or a full prediction of experimental separation performance.

The plotted 1/T dependence belongs to this ZPE-only approximation. At temperatures where vibrational excitation matters, use full partition functions for a quantitative equilibrium prediction.

Constants, compact equation & references

h = 6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ J s; NA = 6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹; kB = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹; c = 299792458 m s⁻¹. These SI defining values are exact. R = NAkB.

Equivalent expression used for the calculation:
ln αB/A = [ℏ/(2kBT)] (√FB − √FA) (1/√ML − 1/√MH).

For multiple independent modes, sum their ΔΔG contributions before exponentiating. A general normal-mode treatment may require masses that differ between states, beyond this calculator’s inputs.

References: NIST physical constants; IUPAC: isotopic fractionation factor. The approximation and the B/A convention are specified above.
